HEENEY, AND THEN EVERYONE ELSE

Isaac Heeney's 3.03 WAR is the standout number in the competition. He leads his nearest rival by 0.59 wins. That gap between first and second is bigger than the gap between second and eighth. AFL.com.au have him third. The model says it is not close, and the numbers back it up. He is averaging career highs across disposals, tackles and goals while playing the highest-value midfield role in the competition by post-clearance equity. There is no comparable player in this dataset.

THE DAICOS PROBLEM

Nick Daicos is ranked first by the panel and 83rd out of 503 AFL players by WAR. That is not a marginal disagreement. That is the model saying 82 players have contributed more wins than the competition's supposed number one this season.

The explanation is not that Daicos is bad. A 1.22 WAR is a genuine starter-level season. The problem is that his 34.7 disposals per game, which drives most of his media profile, does not look as impressive once you control for contested work rate, disposal quality and score creation. He gets the ball, predominantly in space, and the model gives less credit for that than the press box does. Whether the model is right is a legitimate debate. But the gap between #1 and #83 is too large to dismiss.

The five players the model says do not belong in the top 25: Daicos (#83), Neale (#81), Butters (#71), Serong (#67), Anderson (#66). All are producing starter-level WAR. All are being ranked in the top 25 by the panel on reputation or raw disposal volume.

MCCLUGGAGE AT #343

Hugh McCluggage ranks 343rd in the competition with a WAR of 0.16. That is barely above fringe level. AFL.com.au have him 21st. His numbers this season, averaging 17 disposals since returning from injury, represent a dramatic fall from 2025 when he ranked third overall in Champion Data ratings. The model can't account for reputation or injury context. It only sees what is happening on the field, and what is happening on the field in 2026 is not a top-25 player.

THE PLAYERS THE PANEL MISSED

Fourteen of the WAR top 25 are not in AFL.com.au's list at all. Some of them are genuinely surprising.

Nick Blakey
Sydney · Gen. Defender · WAR Rank #2
2.44 WAR
The second-best player in the competition by this model. 26 disposals, 7 rebound 50s and 6.3 intercepts per game. All elite for a general defender. Playing out of his skin and barely rating a mention nationally.
Callum Wilkie
St Kilda · Key Defender · WAR Rank #4
2.23 WAR
9.1 intercepts and 5.3 rebound 50s per game. The model rates him the best key defender in the competition. He does not kick bags or accumulate glamour possessions, so he goes unnoticed by the panel. WAR does not have that blind spot.
Shaun Mannagh
Geelong · Gen. Forward · WAR Rank #10
1.98 WAR
Ranked tenth in the competition. Mannagh is having a career year for the Cats and is almost completely absent from the national conversation. He and Charlie Cameron (#16) are the two biggest general forward omissions.
Chad Warner
Sydney · Mid-Forward · WAR Rank #13
1.92 WAR
Dropped from the media's list after a strong 2025. The model says he is still producing at the same level, with elite post-clearance equity and the highest metres-gained average of any mid-forward in the competition.

WHAT BOTH LISTS AGREE ON

Where the model and panel converge is telling. Heeney as elite, Watson comfortably inside the top five, Treacy, Jackson and Petracca all above the 2.0 threshold. Those are the players whose contributions show up clearly in every measure. Harris Andrews sits 15th in both lists, which is probably the most surprising agreement given he is a defender in a Lions side going through a rough stretch.

Brodie Grundy is worth a final mention. The panel has him 25th, the last man in. WAR has him 15th. His hitout dominance within the ruck pool is being systematically underweighted by a press box that does not look at position-adjusted numbers. He is almost certainly a better player than his media ranking suggests.
