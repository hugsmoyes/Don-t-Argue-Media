WAR VS THE MEDIA:
WHERE DO THEY REALLY RANK?

WAR v36  ·  669 players ranked  ·  AFL.com.au The 25, October edition

AFL.com.au ranks the competition's best 25 players every month, and the October edition is their final word on the 2026 season. It is a good list, put together by people who watch every game. But when you run those same 25 names through the WAR model against all 669 players who took the field this year, some of the results are hard to explain. Not by a spot or two. By more than 200 places.

Below is every player from AFL.com.au's October top 25, their end-of-season WAR, and exactly where they rank in the full competition.

Where the model and the panel agree

The top of both lists looks similar. Four of the panel's top five, Daicos, Bontempelli, Jackson and Watson, sit inside the WAR top 10. Bailey Smith is 14th on the panel and 13th by WAR. Twelve of the 25 names appear on both lists, and all twelve finish inside the WAR top 21.

Luke Jackson

Jackson is third on the panel and first by WAR. That is the two systems agreeing he belongs in the top three and differing only on the order. Twenty-seven games of ruck work, clearances and tackling pressure give him the biggest season total in the competition at 5.38.

Nick Daicos

Daicos is first on the panel and 10th by WAR. His 4.36 is an Elite-tier season, and per game he ranks seventh in the competition. He averaged 34.7 disposals, the most of any player in the league.

What keeps him out of the top few is what the model always finds with Daicos. Much of that volume is won in space, which the model credits at a lower rate than contested ball and direct scoring. This is a disagreement about order, not about whether he is a great player.

Errol Gulden

Gulden is seventh on the panel and 235th by WAR, the biggest gap on the list. Part of it is availability. He played 13 games, and because WAR accumulates, every missed game costs him directly.

The per-game numbers do not close the gap either. Among the 472 players with 10 or more games, Gulden's WAR per game ranks 143rd. He averaged 32.0 disposals, the third most in the competition, with 0.3 goals a game. The panel is rewarding the ball winning. The model wants more of it turned into scores and contested wins.

Zak Butters and Sam Walsh

Butters is fourth on the panel and 144th by WAR. Walsh is 22nd and 143rd. They are the clearest example of where the two systems split. Both average around 29 disposals, both kick about 0.3 goals a game, and both finish on 2.16 WAR.

Walsh played 25 games, so availability is not his issue. His WAR per game ranks 191st among players with 10 or more games. Butters ranks 76th per game from 17 games. High-volume midfielders who rarely hit the scoreboard are where the panel and the model disagree most.

Nasiah Wanganeen-Milera

Wanganeen-Milera is ninth on the panel and 46th by WAR, but he is a different case. Per game he ranks sixth in the competition. He played 17 games, and his total reflects the games he missed rather than how well he played in the ones he did.

Five of the panel's 25 finish outside the WAR top 50: Gulden (#235), Butters (#144), Walsh (#143), Rankine (#67) and Curnow (#58).

The players the panel missed

Thirteen of the WAR top 25 do not appear in The 25. James Sicily ranks fifth by WAR, Shaun Mannagh sixth and Charlie Cameron seventh, and none of them made the list. Darcy Wilmot (#12), Tom Stewart (#14), Josh Rachele (#15) and Shannon Neale (#16) were left off too.

Seventeen of the panel's 25 are midfielders. In the WAR top 25 there are seven. The players the panel missed are defenders and forwards whose value comes from intercepts, rebound 50s, scoring and pressure rather than disposal counts. The panel is not making an irrational choice by favouring midfielders. It is making a systematic one, and the model is designed to correct for it.
