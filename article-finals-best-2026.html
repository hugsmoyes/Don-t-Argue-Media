The team of the finals

Picked the same way as our All-Australian side: three key defenders in the back line, three general defenders on the half-back line, the best ruck, five midfielders, then forwards by position, with the best four left over on the bench. Every spot goes to the highest finals WAR at that position.

Brisbane have nine players in it and Fremantle eight. That is mostly a numbers thing. WAR adds up over games, and the two Grand Finalists played four finals each while most sides played one or two. Sicily, Watson and Moore made it from two games because their per-game numbers were the best of the series. Sicily's 0.252 a game is the highest of anyone who played more than once.

Jackson captains it. His 0.875 finals WAR is 0.167 clear of Zorko, he was in the top three in three of his four finals, and his 0.219 a game is the best of anyone who played all four. Charlie Cameron kicked 11 goals, the most of the finals, and makes the bench.

How this was worked out

Each player's game is scored with the same WAR v36 model used across the site, measured against the 2025 home and away baseline, so one game is worth roughly what one game is worth in the season ratings. Most inputs come straight from the match: goals, score involvements, contested marks, score launches, intercepts, intercept marks, tackles, hitouts to advantage, rebound 50s, one percenters and clangers.

A few of the model's stats are not published for single games. For those we use the player's 2026 rate and apply it to what he did in the final. Threat rating is scaled by kicks, net metres by metres gained, hard ball gets by contested possessions and stoppage clearances by total clearances. Shot quality ratings and inside 50 kick retention use season values. So treat close calls, anything within a couple of hundredths, as close calls.

Match data: Wheelo Ratings and AFL Tables. WAR: Don't Argue Media, v36.
