WHY WAR RATES
THESE SEVEN NO. 1

WAR v36  ·  Positional leaders  ·  2026 home and away season

The WAR model compares players within their position group and accumulates with games played. A key forward is never judged against a general defender, only against other key forwards. That is what makes the positional leaders worth examining individually. These seven players finished the 2026 home and away season as the standout in their role, measured against every comparable player in the competition.

Key Forward  ·  #1

Shannon Neale

Geelong
3.72
WAR (23 games)

Neale finishes the home and away season as the best key forward in the competition, edging Josh Treacy by 0.19 WAR. Forty-seven goals from 23 games is a strong return, but the shape of his game is what separates him.

He sits at the 97th percentile among key forwards for both tackles inside 50 and pressure acts, numbers you would usually expect from a small forward rather than a tall. His xScore rating, set shot rating and ball use equity all sit at the 93rd percentile or higher, so when the ball comes to him, he converts it and uses it well.

A key forward who scores, competes at ground level and brings defensive pressure is exactly the profile the model is built to reward. Neale finishes 20th in the competition overall with 3.72 WAR.

Gen. Forward  ·  #1

Nick Watson

Hawthorn
4.32
WAR (21 games)

Watson is the closest call of the seven. He finishes fourth in the competition and 0.01 WAR ahead of Geelong's Shaun Mannagh, having played two fewer games. Per game the gap is clear: 0.21 WAR to Mannagh's 0.19.

Fifty-five goals from 21 games, 2.6 per game, puts him at the top of his position for scoring. He crumbs at the 99th percentile, wins ground balls at the 97th, and his ball use equity of 7.9 is the best of any general forward. His score involvement rate of 26.4 percent sits at the 99th percentile.

The one gap is pressure. His 10.2 pressure acts per game sit at the 12th percentile for his position, but pressure acts are not one of the 21 WAR inputs, which is why it costs him so little.

Midfielder  ·  #1

Isaac Heeney

Sydney
4.94
WAR (20 games)

Heeney is the best player in the competition, and he did it in 20 games. His 4.94 WAR is 0.45 clear of Marcus Bontempelli, the biggest gap at any position, and his 0.25 WAR per game is the highest rate of any player who played 10 or more matches.

Thirty-two goals from the midfield, 1.6 per game, sits at the top of his position, as does his post-clearance equity. He is at the 97th percentile among midfielders for threat rating, the 96th for contested possessions and the 94th for clearances.

He wins the ball at the coalface, moves it forward and finishes. There is not a relevant part of the midfield role where he falls below the top fifth of the competition.

Mid-Forward  ·  #1

Zac Bailey

Brisbane
3.81
WAR (22 games)

Bailey edges St Kilda's Max Hall by 0.03 WAR to finish as the number one mid-forward and 15th in the competition overall. He played 22 games, kicked 31 goals and averaged 21.7 disposals.

He sits at the 99th percentile among mid-forwards for post-clearance equity, and the 96th for both goals and metres gained. His clearance numbers, at 3.0 per game, are useful rather than dominant, and that is not his job.

Midfield ball winning combined with scoring from the forward half is what the mid-forward role asks for, and nobody did it better this season.

Ruck  ·  #1

Luke Jackson

Fremantle
4.35
WAR (23 games)

Jackson finishes third in the competition with 4.35 WAR and is the best ruck by 0.41 from Max Gawn. Both played all 23 games, so the gap is about output, not availability.

He averages 25.3 hitouts per game with 7.4 to advantage, 4.9 clearances and 5.3 tackles, and kicked 24 goals on top of it. Among rucks he is at the 97th percentile for ground ball gets, tackles and pressure acts, and the 95th for post-clearance equity and disposals.

He wins the ruck contest, wins the clearance and then stays involved. Twenty-three games at that level is why he finishes first.

Key Defender  ·  #1

James Sicily

Hawthorn
4.15
WAR (22 games)

Sicily finishes sixth in the competition and is the best key defender by 0.32 WAR from Callum Wilkie. He averages 8.3 intercepts and 2.3 intercept marks per game, with 5.0 rebound 50s.

He sits at the 99th percentile among key defenders for intercepts and score launches, and the 98th for rebound 50s and net metres gained. He wins the ball back and then starts the attack.

His spoils and one percenters are low for a key defender, at the 4th and 6th percentiles. Sicily's value comes from intercepting and moving the ball rather than spoiling it, and the model rewards that.

Gen. Defender  ·  #1

Tom Stewart

Geelong
3.75
WAR (21 games)

Stewart edges Brisbane's Darcy Wilmot by 0.01 WAR, and he did it in 21 games to Wilmot's 23. Per game the gap is wider, 0.18 WAR to 0.16.

He averages 7.8 intercepts per game, the most of any general defender, with 2.4 intercept marks at the 99th percentile and 5.9 rebound 50s at the 97th. His one percenters sit at the 91st percentile and his score launches at the 95th.

His pressure acts and score involvement are low, at the 7th and 22nd percentiles, which fits the role. Stewart's job is to win the ball back and drive it out of defence, and nobody at his position did that better.
