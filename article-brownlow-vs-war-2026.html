BROWNLOW VS WAR:
WHO DID THE UMPIRES MISS?

WAR v36  ·  2026 Brownlow Medal  ·  668 players

Nick Daicos won the 2026 Brownlow with a record 47 votes. It was a great season and nobody is arguing he shouldn't have a medal. But the Brownlow and WAR are measuring different things, and putting them side by side shows exactly where the umpires look and where they don't.

This one is a fair fight. The Brownlow only counts home and away games, and so does our WAR, so every player is measured over the same matches.

Where they agree

At the top, mostly. Bontempelli is third in the count and second by WAR. Bailey Smith, Max Gawn and Daicos all sit inside the WAR top 11, and Heeney, who WAR has as the best player in the competition, still polled 26 votes from 20 games.

Daicos is the interesting one. Forty-seven votes is the most ever, and WAR has him seventh. That gap is the same one we found in WAR vs The Media. He wins a huge amount of the ball, 34.9 disposals a game, and umpires notice the player who has it most. WAR rates him as Elite, just not the clear best.

Where they don't

Zak Butters polled 26 votes, equal eighth, and ranks 125th by WAR. Errol Gulden got 17 votes from 11 games and ranks 256th. Harley Reid (15 votes, #110) and Brodie Grundy (16 votes, #95) are in the same boat. All four are high-possession players, and possessions are what umpires see.

Charlie Cameron kicked 50 goals and finished 12th by WAR. He didn't poll a single vote.

Cameron isn't alone. Four of the WAR top 25 got zero votes: Cameron, Bayley Fritsch, Mac Andrew and Harris Andrews. Ten of the 25 polled five or fewer. James Sicily is the sixth-best player in the competition by WAR and got three votes. Nick Watson kicked 55 goals and got nine.

Where the votes go

This is the real story. Midfielders got 62% of all Brownlow votes in 2026. By WAR they produce about 27% of the value on the ground. Defenders got 8% of the votes and produce 32% of the value.

The coaches' votes are a useful middle ground. Both senior coaches vote after every game, watching the whole ground from the box, and they gave defenders 20% of their votes, more than double what the umpires did. WAR lines up more closely with the coaches too, with a rank correlation of 0.79 against 0.61 for the Brownlow.

None of this is a knock on the umpires. They are asked to pick the three best players while running around officiating, and the player with the ball is the easiest to see. A defender who reads the play and takes the intercept mark before the ball reaches the forward line is doing his job well, and he is much harder to notice.

How this works

WAR share is each position's share of positive WAR across all 668 players, so players below replacement level don't drag a position down. Coaches votes are the AFL Coaches Association votes from the same home and away games. Rank correlation compares the order of players on each list, where 1 means identical and 0 means no relationship.

Brownlow votes: AFL Tables and AFL.com.au. WAR: Don't Argue Media, v36, 2026 home and away season.
