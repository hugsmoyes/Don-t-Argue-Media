Brisbane  ·  W 106–75 vs Gold Coast
Logan Morris 0.417
17d  ·  7g  ·  4 cont. marks  ·  15 si

Seven goals and the game was done by halftime. Morris was relentless. Fifteen score involvements and four contested marks show he was making himself available and winning the ball in traffic, not just getting on the end of gifts. Brisbane were dominant and Morris was the clearest single reason why.

Sydney  ·  W 104–102 vs St Kilda
Isaac Heeney 0.303
30d  ·  2g  ·  8 cl  ·  6t  ·  5 ic

The Swans needed everything from Heeney in a game that went to the wire, and he delivered it. Eight clearances, six tackles, five intercepts and two goals from 30 disposals. He was working at both ends all night against a side that refused to concede. Two points separated the teams at the final siren. Heeney was the reason Sydney were on the right side of them.

Fremantle  ·  W 155–31 vs North Melbourne
Luke Jackson 0.303
19d  ·  3g  ·  4 cl  ·  13 si  ·  5 sl

Identical WAR to Heeney, in a completely different kind of game. Jackson's 13 score involvements and five score launches were the engine behind a 124-point win. Three goals from the ruck, four clearances and consistent presence wherever North Melbourne tried to compete. He dominated the contest that Fremantle used to dominate everything else.

Adelaide  ·  W 75–74 vs Geelong
Jordan Dawson 0.268
22d  ·  3g  ·  9t  ·  5 ic  ·  3 icm

The most under-reported performance of the round. Three goals, nine tackles, five intercepts and three intercept marks in a one-point win. Dawson covered both ends of the ground in a game that came down to the final siren. Daniel Curtin's 26 disposals and five clearances were important. Dawson's was more important.

Western Bulldogs  ·  W 77–71 vs Hawthorn
Marcus Bontempelli 0.221
24d  ·  2g  ·  7t  ·  6 cl  ·  4 ic

Ed Richards had 28 disposals and seven clearances. Bontempelli had two goals, seven tackles and four intercepts alongside 24 disposals and six clearances. Richards had the busier box score. Bontempelli had the more complete one. He was at both ends of a six-point win that required something from him in every quarter.

Collingwood  ·  L 75–83 vs Melbourne
Nick Daicos 0.197
35d  ·  2g  ·  6 cl  ·  5 ic  ·  9 si

Thirty-five disposals, two goals and six clearances from the round's busiest ball-winner. Daicos was everywhere in a losing side and a 0.197 WAR in defeat is a strong individual contribution. Melbourne's collective output was better across four quarters. Daicos gave Collingwood every opportunity. They didn't take enough of them.

Hawthorn  ·  L 71–77 vs Western Bulldogs
Jai Newcombe 0.194
36d  ·  9 cl  ·  8 ic  ·  10 fp  ·  3t

Nick Watson kicked three goals and generated the headlines. Newcombe's 36 disposals, nine clearances, eight intercepts and ten first possessions kept Hawthorn in the game long enough to make it a contest. Watson was dangerous. Newcombe was indispensable. Neither was enough in a six-point loss to the Bulldogs at the MCG.

St Kilda  ·  L 102–104 vs Sydney
Liam Ryan 0.193
12d  ·  5g  ·  9 si

Five goals and nine score involvements in a two-point loss. Ryan gave St Kilda every chance to win and came within the width of a post of taking it. He did his job at an elite level from limited touches, generated the scoring opportunities that kept St Kilda in the game all night, and still walked off the wrong side of the result. A performance that deserved better.

Geelong  ·  L 74–75 vs Adelaide
Sam De Koning 0.176
25d  ·  11 cl  ·  5 ic  ·  15 fp  ·  4t

Fifteen first possessions, eleven clearances and five intercepts in a one-point loss. De Koning was winning ball all day, at stoppages, at ground level, in the ruck contest, and kept giving Geelong opportunities they could not take. The final siren came one kick too soon and he was the best player Geelong had on a day that hurt.

Carlton  ·  W 72–67 vs Essendon
Harry McKay 0.175
16d  ·  3g  ·  5 cl  ·  2 cm  ·  6 si

Three goals and two contested marks in a five-point win. McKay did it from 16 disposals. He wasn't everywhere. He was in the right place, repeatedly. George Hewett's 27-disposal game drove the midfield. McKay's goals were the moments that settled it. Carlton needed both and got both.

Port Adelaide  ·  W 77–71 vs West Coast
Jason Horne-Francis 0.158
18d  ·  3g  ·  4 cl  ·  3t  ·  12 pa

Three goals from the mid-forward in a six-point win that was tighter than Port would have liked. Horne-Francis playing forward from the midfield gave West Coast a matchup problem they never solved. His scoring and contested work in the middle of the ground made him the most influential Power player on the day.

Melbourne  ·  W 83–75 vs Collingwood
Kysaiah Pickett 0.151
28d  ·  3g  ·  5 cl  ·  4 ic  ·  18 pa

Three goals, five clearances and sustained pressure across four quarters of a game Melbourne were never fully comfortable in. Pickett's third-quarter work kept Collingwood at arm's length. He gave Melbourne a forward option and a defensive presence across the same stretch of the game, and the Pies found no answer for either.

Essendon  ·  L 67–72 vs Carlton
Sam Durham 0.146
30d  ·  1g  ·  5t  ·  27 pa  ·  3 cl

Thirty disposals, five tackles and 27 pressure acts in a five-point loss. Durham was Essendon's best player across four quarters and gave his side a real shot at winning. Carlton's structure held, but this was not a game Essendon lost through lack of effort. Durham carried their midfield and came up just short.

Gold Coast  ·  L 75–106 vs Brisbane
Ben King 0.126
8d  ·  4g  ·  6 si

Four goals from eight disposals in a game that had been decided by halftime. The supply from Gold Coast's midfield dried up early and the scoreline reflects a heavy afternoon for the Suns. King kept working through it, kept finding ways to score, and reminded anyone paying attention what he is capable of when the ball actually gets near him.

North Melbourne  ·  L 31–155 vs Fremantle
George Wardlaw 0.106
11d  ·  1g  ·  7t  ·  7 cl  ·  23 pa

Seven tackles, seven clearances and 23 pressure acts in a game that was settled inside the first quarter. Wardlaw kept working in a 124-point defeat when most of his teammates had nowhere to go, and carried North Melbourne's competitive effort almost entirely by himself. There isn't much to say about the afternoon. His numbers say enough.

West Coast  ·  L 71–77 vs Port Adelaide
Harley Reid 0.102
25d  ·  7 cl  ·  5t  ·  22 pa  ·  3 ic

Seven clearances, five tackles and 22 pressure acts in a six-point loss at Optus Stadium. Reid was West Coast's most complete contributor across all four quarters, at the stoppage, through the middle, in his defensive pressure. He continues to carry a midfield with very little depth behind him. The Eagles keep losing close games.
