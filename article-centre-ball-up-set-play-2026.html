SET PLAYS AREN'T.

Every 2025 and 2026 match  ·  434 games  ·  home and away plus finals

Across sport, teams are chasing what they can control. Open play is full of variance. Set plays can be drilled and measured. The EPL is obsessed with them. Arsenal just won the title with 28 set-piece goals, including a record 17 from corners. About 40% of their league goals came from dead balls.

The AFL's version? I think it's the centre ball-up. Same spot, both teams set, fully drillable. And after the 2026 rule changes, it's more of a set play than it has ever been.

The centre is now a set play

Two rule changes reshaped the centre in 2026. The bounce is gone, so every centre restart is a ball-up. And rucks can't cross the centre circle line before they contest. The AFL found that only 21% of centre ruck contests in 2025 had even one ruck jumping. Most were wrestles.

The result is a cleaner, more predictable tap, and something mids can run off. Centre ball-up chains rose from 13.5% to 14.9% of all points, and each centre ball-up was worth 0.91 points across both teams, up from 0.81. There were also more of them: 29.25 per match, up from 28.00, because more goals means more restarts.

Turnovers are still the biggest source of points by a distance. The centre is a minority of scoring. But it's the one source a team can set up, rehearse and repeat, and it grew.

Clearances turn into territory

Winning the ball out of the centre moves the game. Each extra centre clearance a team won in a match was worth about 1.2 more inside 50s, and 1.1 more points from centre chains. Teams that won the centre clearance count also won the inside 50 count 66% of the time.

It shows up in results. Teams that won the centre clearance count won 62.8% of games in 2026, up from 57.6%.

Quality beats quantity

But winning the ball isn't the whole story. Brisbane won the centre clearance count in only 59% of their games and still won the flag. What set them apart was what happened next: they turned each centre clearance into 1.25 points, the best in the comp and close to double their 0.69 in 2025. Their centre ball-up points went from 8.3 to 18.6 per game.

Melbourne were the opposite case. They won the centre clearance count in 67% of games, more than anyone, and won only half of those.

What these set plays reward

Leaping, mobile rucks. With the line rule, hitouts to advantage rose across the board, and rucks' share of centre clearances jumped from 14% to 19%. Rucks who jump and direct the ball now beat rucks who wrestle.

Ruck depth. Two-ruck setups, where both rucks attend at least 20% of centre ball-ups, went from 39% to 66% of team-games. A second ruck who can really compete is now a list need.

Burst and first possession. A cleaner tap rewards mids who run onto it at speed and get out of the square quickly.

What happens next. Brisbane's edge was points per clearance, not clearance volume. The next one or two possessions matter as much as the ball-winner.

The best mids own the square, and the mix follows a pattern: a defensive cover (Berry, Brayshaw, Nash), a ball-winner (Newcombe, Serong, Butters, Anderson), and a quick, damaging offensive mid (Bolton, Warner, Petracca).

The set-up barely changes between teams either. An anchor behind the circle, a neutral mid to the ruck's side, and a spear in front.

What if that changes? Attending the ball-up doesn't mean you have to chase the ball. What do two anchors look like? What could a team exploit?

2026 brought two big rule changes: last disposal, and rucks held behind the line at centre ball-ups. Teams exploited the first quickly, letting the ball run out untouched to win a free kick. Nobody clearly exploited the second. Is 2027 the year someone does?

My crazy theory: when you're protecting a late lead, a clean centre clearance and a quick inside 50 is the worst result. So give away a free kick in the ruck contest on purpose. Step over the line, slow it down, flood your defensive 50.

This year's Grand Final showed why. Fremantle led by three goals with four minutes left. Brisbane won clean centre clearances twice in a row and got straight back into it.

Endless possibilities. And for a stats nerd like me, endless edges to find.
